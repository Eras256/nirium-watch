The problem

An endpoint can answer 402 and still fail to collect. Nobody notices until an integrator debugs it by hand.

This already happened on Stellar: the OpenZeppelin facilitator's issue #47 was a roughly 32-day outage, found by four separate integrators debugging it independently. We were burned too - a payment method we listed as "live" had zero settled payments, and nothing caught it.

How it works

  • Pay like an agent - every 30 minutes, probe each endpoint with no payment, expect 402, then pay with our own testnet key through Nirium's x402 client.
  • Verify on-chain - call Stellar RPC getTransaction(hash) and confirm a real transfer moved the required asset to the required address, for the required amount.
  • Pin a signed receipt - hash the round's results, sign them with our monitor key, and pin that record (Nirium Audit Trail's nirium-audit-v1 format) to IPFS - one receipt per round.
  • Alert on change - when an endpoint goes from settled to failing, or back, send one HMAC-signed alert to Pullcord. No repeats while a failure persists.

  • No partnerships. None with OpenZeppelin, Rail402 or AgentSmith. We only call their public testnet endpoints.

  • Pullcord is our own earlier project too. It existed before this event; only its external alert intake route was added during HackMeridian, in Pullcord's own repo, so Nirium Watch could send it alerts.
  • Testnet only. Every payment uses our own testnet key. No mainnet secret exists anywhere in this project.
  • One failed check is not proof a facilitator is "down." This page shows history, not verdicts.

FAQ

What does "settled" mean here?

The endpoint answered 402, we paid with our own testnet key, and we found a matching transfer to the required address, for the required amount, on Stellar's RPC. Not just a 200 response.

Why these three facilitators?

They are the x402 facilitators on Stellar testnet we could actually call. SCF round 45 funded three: Rail402, AgentSmith and Rumble Fish. We check the first two, plus OpenZeppelin, which runs the facilitator our own Nirium agent already uses. Rumble Fish is not checked yet: on 26-oct-2026 its public repo was still a skeleton, with no testnet facilitator to call, and we'll add it when one is live. Rail402 has its own hosted demo seller. For OpenZeppelin we check through our Nirium testnet agent, and for AgentSmith we built our own small seller.

Is any of this real money?

No. Every payment is testnet USDC paid from our own buyer key. Nothing here moves real funds.

What is Nirium?

Nirium is our own prior work: an x402 toolkit for Stellar and an audit trail service, both built before this event. We use them here; we did not build them during HackMeridian. More at nirium.xyz.

What is Pullcord?

Pullcord is our own earlier project: alerts for what you hold on Stellar, such as a protocol you use changing or pausing, your tokens being frozen or clawed back, or a payment arriving. It existed before this event. During HackMeridian we added one route to Pullcord's repo, an external alert intake (POST /v1/alerts/external, commit 4f131f2), so Nirium Watch can send it signed alerts. More at pullcord.xyz.

Why would a check fail?

A dead endpoint, a facilitator outage, a discovered route that needs parameters we do not send, or a testnet reset or upgrade. Failures stay on the page as real history, not hidden.

Why does one check take several seconds?

"Check time" is the whole round trip for one endpoint: ask for the 402, pay, wait for the payment to settle on Stellar, then read the transaction back from Stellar RPC and verify it. It is not the endpoint's response time.

Why did receipts stop going through Nirium's Audit Trail?

Receipts up to 2026-10-26 08:09 UTC were anchored through Nirium's Audit Trail service, one per endpoint check. From the 08:11 UTC round on, we sign and pin each round's receipt directly to our own Pinata account - same record format, one receipt per round instead of one per endpoint. The Audit Trail pins every anchored receipt to Nirium's shared Pinata account, and at our check volume we were on pace to push that account toward its pin limit, which would have broken anchoring for everyone using it, not just us.
