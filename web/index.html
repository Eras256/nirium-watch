Uptime and settlement checks for x402 APIs on Stellar

Nirium Watch pays each endpoint the way an agent would, on testnet, then confirms on chain that the money actually moved — not just that a 402 came back.

See live status View source

The problem

  • Answering 402 Payment Required proves nothing about whether you can actually get paid. A facilitator can stay broken for weeks while every endpoint behind it keeps returning a perfect 402.
  • On Stellar this already happened: the OpenZeppelin facilitator's issue #47 — a ~32-day outage diagnosed by hand by four separate integrators.
  • We were burned ourselves: a payment method we listed as "live" had zero settled payments, and nothing caught it.

How it works

  • Probe — GET the endpoint with no payment. Expect 402 and parse the requirement: scheme, network, asset, amount, payTo.
  • Pay — fetch again with Nirium's x402 client and our own testnet buyer key. Expect 200 and a payment-response header with a transaction hash.
  • Verify on-chain — call Stellar RPC getTransaction(hash). Did it succeed, and did a SAC transfer event actually move the asset to payTo for the required amount?
  • Signed receipt — hash the result, sign it with our monitor key, anchor it to the Nirium Audit Trail as an IPFS CID.

Prior art

Nearest prior art we found: carstenjacobsen/x402-testing-tool (Stellar Hacks: Agents), which mocks endpoints and clients for development. Nirium Watch checks live endpoints continuously and verifies settlement on chain — a neighbour, not a duplicate.

Honest limits

  • All payments are ours. The buyer key is our own team key. This is not adoption, not external traffic.
  • No partnerships. None with OpenZeppelin, Rail402 or AgentSmith; we only call their public testnet endpoints. Nirium is our own prior work (SDK + Audit Trail), disclosed.
  • A failed check is not proof a facilitator is "down." One failure just means one failed check at one timestamp; this page shows history, not verdicts.
  • Testnet only. No mainnet secret exists anywhere in this project.
  • Rail402 Bazaar discovery is called literally. A couple of discovered resources are templated or need query params we don't always supply; those show up honestly as down rather than being special-cased.
